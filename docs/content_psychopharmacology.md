# Content Psychopharmacology

A framework for analyzing, strategizing, and ideating content based on the emotional transactions viewers seek from media.

## Core premise

People consume non-utility content to regulate their emotional state. Not sometimes — as the default behavior. Watching a video is a deliberate (often unconscious) act of self-medication. The viewer is moving from one felt state to another, and the content is the delivery mechanism.

Grounded in Zillmann's Mood Management Theory (1988), Tamir's instrumental emotion regulation (2016), Loewenstein's information gap theory, Berger's STEPPS virality research, Ryan & Deci's self-determination theory, and state-specific literature.

Content is psychoactive. The thumbnail is the pill bottle. The title is the label. The first three seconds are onset. The video body is the active dose. Design accordingly.

---

## The fifteen states

Fifteen discrete emotional transactions viewers seek from content. Each defined by a start state, an end state, and a set of behavioral properties.

### 1. Vindication
Unseen, privately uncertain → confirmed, seen, correct. Need: esteem, identity coherence. Literature: Festinger, Oyserman, Tajfel. The "I was right all along" payoff.

### 2. Controlled Outrage
Ambient frustration → righteous anger at an external target. Need: group belonging, moral identity. Literature: Crockett, Tamir, Haidt. The single most viral emotion online.

### 3. Awe / Elevation
Ordinary, depleted → expanded, humbled, moved. Need: self-transcendence, meaning. Literature: Keltner & Haidt, Algoe & Haidt. Berger found awe the strongest predictor of share behavior in NYT data.

### 4. Competence / Insight
Under-stimulated, pre-task → sharper, holding new mental model. Need: mastery, autonomy. Literature: Ryan & Deci, Csikszentmihalyi, Loewenstein.

### 5. Controlled Fear / Vigilance
Uneasy → informed, vigilant, prepared. Need: safety, perceived control. Literature: Tamir (instrumental fear), Gerbner, Zuckerman.

### 6. Schadenfreude / Downward Comparison
Inadequate, resentful → relieved, superior. Need: esteem, relative status. Literature: Wills, Smith, Leach. "At least I'm not that."

### 7. Amusement / Play
Bored, mildly stressed → entertained, relaxed. Need: play, cognitive variety. Literature: Berger (STEPPS), Martin, Berlyne. Arguably the most-shared category.

### 8. Catharsis
Emotionally congested → released, purged. Need: emotional processing. Literature: Zillmann (sad-film paradox), Oliver, Aristotle. The release itself is the pleasure.

### 9. Nostalgia
Dislocated, meaning-deficient → grounded, self-continuous. Need: meaning, temporal belonging. Literature: Batcho, Routledge, Wildschut, Sedikides.

### 10. Belonging / Parasocial Warmth
Lonely, socially depleted → among-one's-people. Need: relatedness. Literature: Ryan & Deci, Horton & Wohl, Baumeister & Leary. The audience-building state.

### 11. Escape / Absorption
Aversive present → attention displaced. Need: tension release, self-respite. Literature: Zillmann (absorption potential), Nell, Csikszentmihalyi.

### 12. Calm / Tranquility
Overstimulated → baseline. Need: homeostasis, safety. Literature: Zillmann (arousal reduction), Reiss.

### 13. Aspiration / Possible Self
Dissatisfied → inhabiting ideal self. Need: growth, self-actualization. Literature: Bandura, Markus & Nurius, Higgins, Oyserman.

### 14. Sensual / Sexual Arousal
Under-stimulated → activated. Need: physical pleasure, romantic fantasy. Literature: Janssen & Bancroft, Paasonen.

### 15. Curiosity / Information Gap
Incomplete → resolved. Need: cognitive closure. Literature: Loewenstein, Berlyne, Kang et al.

## The three axes

Each state has three independent behavioral properties that determine how it performs under different distribution contexts.

### Axis 1 — Induction speed
How fast the start state can be created in a viewer who isn't already in it.

- **Instant (1-2 sec)** — thumbnail alone can induce
- **Fast (3-10 sec)** — needs hook line or first-clip investment
- **Slow (10+ sec)** — must be built narratively

### Axis 2 — Context dependence
How much prior state, belief, or relationship the viewer must bring.

- **Low** — works on any stranger
- **Medium** — needs specific pre-held belief or topic interest
- **High** — needs existing relationship, shared reference frame, or active life circumstance

### Axis 3 — Signal compressibility
How much of the state's promise fits in a thumbnail + title at glance speed.

- **High** — the whole transaction implies in a single image
- **Medium** — partial signal, needs title or genre familiarity
- **Low** — unreadable at thumbnail resolution; must earn click on other grounds

|---|---|---|---|

| Competence | Fast | Low-Medium | Medium |

## What the axes reveal

**Curiosity and Sensual are the cleanest cold-start hooks.** Both score Instant / Low / High. They require almost nothing from the viewer, which is also why they get strip-mined.

**Outrage and Fear are nearly as fast but carry tribal baggage.** Instant induction and high compressibility, but context dependence creeps in — the viewer must share the moral frame or perceive the threat as plausible. Works devastatingly inside a tribe, fails outside.

**Schadenfreude is more contextual than it appears.** Instant induction, but the viewer must be in a status-anxious state for the downward comparison to feel rewarding. Thrives in economically precarious audiences, falls flat with secure viewers.

**Competence is more cold-viable than typically credited.** The topic-interest filter is a feature — the viewers it reaches are high-quality. Mechanism-reveal educational channels can start cold without the clickbait arms race.

**Aspiration's compressibility is high.** Transformation thumbnails and luxury signifiers work cold because the visible ideal-self cue does most of the work. Self-discrepancy is near-universal in target demographics.

**Belonging and Catharsis are conversion states, not acquisition states.** Both score Slow / High / Low. They build the most durable audience relationships but can't be the primary hook for cold acquisition. A channel built on Belonging must acquire via some other state first. Structural, not fixable.

**Awe is the outlier.** Slow induction but extraordinary share multiplier when it lands. Low volume, high leverage. The state that produces the biggest share-rate payoffs and the smallest click-through rates.

## Durability

A separate property from the three axes. Describes how long the delivered state lasts after the video ends, which determines what the content is actually building.

- **Spike (seconds–minutes)** — Outrage, Curiosity, Schadenfreude, Sensual, Amusement. Viewer returns to baseline fast. Requires repeat dosing.
- **Mood (minutes–hours)** — Vindication, Awe, Calm, Catharsis, Fear. Persists through subsequent activities.
- **Identity (days–permanent)** — Competence, Aspiration, Belonging, Nostalgia. Alters self-concept or affiliation.

Identity-durable states build audiences and brands. Spike states drive reach but leave no residue. Match durability to the goal — reach/activation plays can run on Spike, brand and audience building require Identity.

## Induction signals

Each Instant-induction state has a characteristic visible cause — the element in the thumbnail or opening that creates the start state in 1-3 seconds.

- **Outrage** → visible villain or moral violation
- **Curiosity** → visible open loop or unanswered question
- **Amusement** → visible incongruity or surprise
- **Schadenfreude** → visible failure or embarrassment
- **Sensual** → visible body
- **Fear** → visible threat

Fast-induction states have weaker but still-identifiable signals:

- **Competence** → visible framework, diagram, or mechanism artifact
- **Vindication** → visible named target or tribal cue
- **Aspiration** → visible transformation or status marker
- **Nostalgia** → visible period-coded object or aesthetic

If a concept claims an Instant or Fast state but has no induction signal, it's not a concept yet — it's a visual.

## Share vs. retain bias

A secondary analytical split that determines what the content is actually building.

**Share-dominant states** (viral distribution, low retention): Outrage, Awe, Amusement, Vindication, Curiosity, Fear. High arousal. Berger's finding.

**Retain-dominant states** (audience loyalty, low viral distribution): Belonging, Escape, Calm, Aspiration, Nostalgia, Sensual, Catharsis, Schadenfreude, Competence. Lower arousal or more private consumption.

Viral channels and audience channels are usually different creators because they optimize for different halves of the taxonomy. The stacking ladder is the mechanism for doing both at once.

## Arrival context

The viewer's arrival context determines which states are viable as primaries. Four contexts, from coldest to warmest:

- **Cold algorithmic feed** — viewer has no relationship, no topic interest, no pre-loaded state. Primary must score Instant or Fast induction + Low or Low-Medium context dependence + High or Medium compressibility.
- **Niche algorithmic feed** — viewer has topic interest but no relationship. Primary can tolerate Medium context dependence. Compressibility slightly less critical.
- **Subscribed feed** — viewer has relationship with creator. Primary can tolerate Slow induction, High context dependence, Low compressibility. The relationship carries the load the thumbnail used to carry.
- **Linked/shared click** — viewer arrived via another person or context. Primary can be almost anything — the referrer did the induction work.

Most concept failures trace to an arrival-context mismatch: using a Slow/High/Low state (Belonging, Catharsis) as a cold-feed primary, or wasting a High/Low/High state (Sensual, Curiosity) on subscribed viewers who would have clicked anyway.

## Stacking

Content almost never delivers a single state. It stacks them — across time and within moments. Two independent dimensions.

### Vertical stacking — the journey across the viewer's experience

Three layers across the viewer's journey with the content:

- **Primary** — the hook. Earns the click. Must match arrival-context requirements.
- **Secondary** — the sustain. Holds watch time through the body.
- **Tertiary** — the residue. Drives return, subscribe, share. Usually Identity-durable.

The algorithmic ladder: primary earns the click, secondary earns the watch, tertiary earns the audience.

**Ceiling effects of single-layer content:**
- Primary-only = viral, no audience (Outrage-only, Curiosity-only clickbait)
- Secondary-only = high retention, no reach (educational content that flatlines under 5K views)
- Tertiary-only = invisible

**Vertical stacking rule:** Primary state's axis scores must match the viewer's arrival context. Secondary and tertiary states have no axis constraint — once the viewer is watching, induction speed and compressibility no longer matter.

**Worked vertical stacks:**

- **Kurzgesagt:** Curiosity → Competence → Awe + Belonging. Cold-hook, held-sustain, Identity-residue.
- **True crime documentary:** Curiosity → Fear → Vindication + Belonging. Same structural pattern, different states.
- **Fitness influencer at scale:** Sensual → Aspiration → Competence + Belonging. Body stops the thumb, possible-self sustains, knowledge and parasocial bond build the channel.
- **Cozy-living channel starting cold:** broken. Primary (Calm or Belonging) scores Slow/High/Low. No viable cold hook. Must acquire via another state first.

### Horizontal stacking — multiple states at the same moment

At any single moment in the content — a thumbnail, a scene, a title, a hook line — multiple states can be delivered simultaneously. This is where most of the real packaging craft lives. The thumbnail inducing Curiosity alone is a baseline competent thumbnail. The thumbnail inducing Curiosity + Sensual + Schadenfreude at the same time is an outlier.

**Why horizontal stacking works:** Each additional state added to the same moment widens the audience the content can hook without narrowing the promise. A pure-Curiosity thumbnail hooks curious viewers. A Curiosity + Sensual thumbnail hooks curious viewers *and* viewers in a sensual-seeking state *and* viewers in both at once. The states don't compete for attention; they compound it.

**The ceiling:** States must be horizontally compatible. Incompatible states cancel each other or produce incoherence the viewer rejects subconsciously. Sensual + Calm at the same moment fails because arousal direction contradicts. Schadenfreude + Awe at the same moment fails because eye-line direction contradicts (looking down vs. looking up). Compatible horizontal stacks reinforce; incompatible ones short-circuit.

**Typical compatible horizontal pairs:**
- Curiosity + anything (Curiosity is the universal horizontal stacker — it layers onto almost every other state without conflict)
- Sensual + Aspiration
- Outrage + Vindication
- Fear + Curiosity
- Amusement + Schadenfreude
- Awe + Competence
- Competence + Vindication
- Aspiration + Competence

**Typical incompatible horizontal pairs:**
- Sensual + Calm (arousal direction conflict)
- Schadenfreude + Awe (eye-line conflict)
- Outrage + Amusement (emotional register conflict, though dark comedy resolves this deliberately)
- Belonging + Schadenfreude (in-group warmth vs. out-group contempt)
- Calm + Fear (autonomic conflict)

**Horizontal stacking across layers — thumbnail, title, and hook:**

Each layer of the package can carry its own horizontal stack. The thumbnail can induce Curiosity + Sensual. The title can promise Curiosity + Vindication. The hook can deliver Curiosity + Fear. The shared element (Curiosity, in this example) is the spine of the concept. The secondary elements (Sensual, Vindication, Fear) vary by layer to maximize the total audience surface the package can reach — while never contradicting the spine.

When the spine isn't shared — when the thumbnail promises Curiosity, the title promises Vindication, and the hook delivers Competence — the concept reads as incoherent even if each layer is individually strong. This is one of the most common failure modes in B2B and brand content: each element was written by someone solving a different problem.

### Bait-and-switch as a horizontal stacking pattern

Bait-and-switch is a horizontal stack where the promised state at one layer differs from the delivered state at another. Thumbnail promises Sensual, video delivers Competence. Title promises Vindication, content delivers Curiosity.

Sometimes this is deliberate craft — the fitness influencer promising Sensual and delivering Aspiration is exploiting the fact that Sensual earns higher click-through than Aspiration in cold feeds, then converting to the Identity-durable state once the viewer is held. This is a high-performing pattern when the two states are adjacent and the viewer experiences the switch as a positive upgrade.

Sometimes it's a failure mode — the thumbnail promises Curiosity and the video delivers nothing, producing the failure pivot (Resentment → unsubscribe). The difference between craft and failure is whether the delivered state is a *better* version of the promised state or a *weaker* one.

### The two dimensions together

Full-package stacking is a 3x3 matrix: vertical layers (Primary/Secondary/Tertiary) × horizontal stacks at each layer. A complete concept names:

- Primary vertical layer: one spine state + horizontal stack of compatible states
- Secondary vertical layer: one sustain state + horizontal stack
- Tertiary vertical layer: one residue state + horizontal stack

Most concepts in the wild fill only the vertical dimension and leave the horizontal dimension to accident. Explicitly naming the horizontal stack at each layer is where the concept gets sharp.

**Worked full-package stack (for a Kurzgesagt-style video):**

- **Primary (thumbnail + title):** Curiosity spine + Awe (visible scale) + Fear (implied threat in the question)
- **Secondary (video body):** Competence spine + Curiosity (continuing loops) + Awe (visuals sustain wonder)
- **Tertiary (post-video residue):** Belonging spine + Competence (durable knowledge) + Awe (residual sense of scale)

Curiosity is the throughline. Each layer adds compatible states without contradicting. The viewer's experience is continuously compounding rather than shifting.

### Stacking integrity

Both vertical and horizontal stacks must maintain integrity.

**Vertical integrity:** The states must be coherent in sequence. Primary → Secondary → Tertiary should feel like a natural progression, not a reset. Sensual (Primary) → Calm (Secondary) breaks because the viewer's body was activated and the sustain asks it to deactivate — incoherent experience.

**Horizontal integrity:** States at the same moment must be compatible (see pairs above). Incompatible horizontal stacks produce incoherence the viewer rejects subconsciously before any conscious decision is made.

**Cross-layer integrity:** The spine state should ideally carry across vertical layers. Changing spines mid-stack is possible but advanced — most successful content uses a single spine (usually Curiosity, Competence, or Aspiration) that runs top-to-bottom, with horizontal companions varying by layer.

## Failure pivots

When a state's promise is broken, the viewer pivots to a specific negative state, not neutral. This is the most useful field for pre-audit review.

- Failed Vindication → Schadenfreude at creator
- Failed Awe → Cynicism, contempt
- Failed Competence → Schadenfreude + Outrage ("fraud")
- Failed Aspiration → Inadequacy → avoidance (viewer leaves, doesn't return)
- Failed Catharsis → Contempt, cringe
- Failed Amusement → Secondhand embarrassment
- Failed Fear → Dismissal, mockery ("fearmonger")
- Failed Curiosity → Resentment (classic clickbait collapse)
- Failed Belonging → Betrayal (strongest negative; parasocial breaks are violent)
- Failed Sensual → Disgust, contempt
- Failed Outrage → Creator fatigue, loss of trust
- Failed Schadenfreude → Self-disgust at having watched

Some failures are recoverable — failed Curiosity damages the video, not the channel. Some are catastrophic — failed Belonging can end a career. Knowing which failure mode a concept risks before building is the point.

## Application: the six framework questions

For any analysis, strategy decision, or ideation concept, the framework collapses to six questions:

1. **What state is the primary transaction?** Name one of the fifteen.
2. **Do its axis scores match the arrival context?** Cold feed needs Instant/Fast + Low/Low-Med + High/Medium. Warmer contexts relax these requirements.
3. **What states are stacked vertically as secondary and tertiary?** Check vertical integrity.
4. **What states are stacked horizontally at each layer?** Check horizontal compatibility and cross-layer spine integrity.
5. **What's the durability target?** Spike, Mood, or Identity — and does it match the strategic goal?
6. **If the primary promise misses, where does the viewer pivot?** Is that failure recoverable or catastrophic?

If all six answers are clean, the concept is framework-legible and ready to develop. If any answer is missing or muddled, the concept has a gap that will show up as underperformance.

## Application: analysis workflow

When analyzing existing content (outliers, competitors, own back catalog):

1. **Identify the primary spine state** from the thumbnail alone, no context. What is the viewer's nervous system being promised?
2. **Identify the horizontal stack** at the thumbnail layer. What additional compatible states are being induced at the same moment?
3. **Identify the induction signal(s).** What visible elements create each state in the horizontal stack?
4. **Predict the secondary and tertiary states** from title + channel context. Does the video likely deliver the stack the hook promised, or is it bait-and-switch?
5. **Score the primary against its arrival context.** Is this a well-matched primary for where the viewer came from?
6. **Flag stacking integrity** — vertical, horizontal, and cross-layer. Do all states reinforce or are there contradictions?
7. **Predict durability.** Is this Spike, Mood, or Identity? What is it building?
8. **Identify the failure pivot** if the primary misses.

Output per piece: primary spine state + horizontal stack at each layer, induction signals, vertical stack (Primary/Secondary/Tertiary), arrival context, integrity verdict, durability, failure pivot.

## Application: ideation workflow

When generating concepts:

1. **Start with arrival context.** Where will this content be seen? This determines viable primary states.
2. **Choose the primary spine state** based on what the content can honestly deliver and what matches the arrival context. Do not pick a primary the content can't sustain.
3. **Name the induction signal** — the specific visible element that will create the start state. If you can't name it, the concept is incomplete.
4. **Add the horizontal stack at the primary layer** — which compatible states can be layered onto the thumbnail and title at the same moment to widen the audience surface without contradicting the spine.
5. **Choose the secondary and tertiary vertical states** that reinforce the primary and, for tertiary, build Identity-durable residue if audience-building is the goal.
6. **Add horizontal stacks at secondary and tertiary layers** — compatible states that compound the experience at each stage.
7. **Check all integrity conditions.** Vertical progression coherent? Horizontal pairs compatible? Spine carrying across layers?
8. **Audit the failure pivot.** If the primary misses, where does the viewer land? Is that acceptable?
9. **Map to visual/structural execution** — this is where psychopharmacology hands off to thumbnail structure (Violation, Contrast, Proof, Reveal, Confrontation, Reaction, Simplification), title craft, and hook writing.

## What the framework replaces

Nothing, entirely. It replaces "emotional" used as a category in strategy conversations. "Make it emotional" has been the level of direction the industry has operated at since Binet & Field. This framework makes emotional targeting *directional* — which state, for which arrival context, at which durability, with which failure risk, compounded across which stacks.

For packaging and ideation specifically, it adds a psychopharmacological vocabulary to the existing visual-structural vocabulary. Structure tells you how the thumbnail works mechanically. State tells you why it works neurochemically. Stacking tells you how multiple states compound within a moment and across a journey. All three are required. None is sufficient.
