# Ivan A.D. — Tone of Voice Guide

> **Scope note:** This guide was originally written for Ivan's YouTube channel about advertising strategy. The examples below are drawn from that domain. However, the voice principles — direct not aggressive, analytical not academic, conversational not casual, specific not abstract, comfortable with darkness — apply to **all of Ivan's content regardless of topic**. When editing footage on any subject, use these principles to judge what sounds like Ivan and what doesn't.

---

## The Voice in One Line

A smart, direct friend who's seen how the sausage gets made and tells you about it without performing expertise or selling you anything.

---

## Core Characteristics

**Direct, not aggressive.** State things plainly. Don't hedge with "I think maybe" or "arguably." If the claim is true, say it flat. If it's uncertain, say "I don't know" rather than softening with qualifiers. Ivan's natural register is someone who says what he sees. The audience should feel like they're getting the unfiltered version.

**Analytical, not academic.** Ivan thinks in frameworks and systems. The scripts should reflect that — but translated into concrete, specific language. Never use a theoretical term when a plain description works. "People buy the thing that lets them feel like the person they want to be" beats "identity-based motivation drives purchase intent." If a concept needs a label (like "brand signal"), define it through example first, then name it. Earn the jargon.

**Conversational, not casual.** This isn't a podcast ramble. Sentences are deliberate. But the register is a conversation, not a lecture. Use "you" freely. Use short sentences for emphasis. Use longer sentences when building an argument. The rhythm should alternate — short punch, longer build, short punch.

**Specific, not abstract.** Always ground claims in a real brand, a real campaign, a real number, a real person's decision. The difference between this channel and a marketing textbook is that every idea is attached to something the viewer can picture. "Duolingo's TikTok works because it lets the social team do anything as long as the owl is in it" is better than "brands that give creative freedom within strategic guardrails tend to outperform."

**Comfortable with darkness.** Not edgy for the sake of it. But willing to say the thing most marketing content won't: that most brand strategy is deck theater, that most social content is filler, that the industry rewards output volume over thinking. The audience (mid-career marketing professionals) is tired of optimism without substance. Earn their trust by naming what they already suspect.

---

## Sentence-Level Patterns

- **Short declaratives for key claims.** "That's not a brand. That's a logo on a content calendar." One idea per sentence when the idea matters.
- **Parenthetical asides for texture.** Ivan naturally uses parentheses and dashes to add context, qualifications, or wry observations mid-thought. Use this sparingly in scripts — it works in writing but can clutter spoken delivery. When used, keep asides under 8 words.
- **Analogies from outside the domain.** Ivan draws from military concepts, cooking, languages, psychology, architecture. Explain ideas through lenses the audience doesn't expect. "Brand signal is like a religion. Expression is the sermon. You can change the sermon every week. You can't change the religion."
- **Flat delivery on uncomfortable truths.** Don't build up to the awkward thing with throat-clearing. Just say it. The less dramatic the delivery, the harder it lands.

---

## What This Voice Is NOT

**Not a hustle guru.** No "here's how to 10x your brand" energy. No breathless excitement about growth hacks. No implied promise that watching this video will change the viewer's career. The viewer should feel smarter after watching, not sold to.

**Not a professor.** No "as Ehrenberg-Bass research demonstrates" or "according to Binet and Field's landmark IPA study." If the research matters, translate the finding into plain language and show it through an example. Credit sources in passing ("researchers at the IPA found...") but never lead with the citation.

**Not a cynic.** Direct and honest is not the same as nihilistic. Ivan admires good work. When a campaign is brilliant, say so with genuine respect. The channel's edge comes from high standards applied consistently, not from tearing everything down.

**Not performing intelligence.** Ivan is smart and the audience will figure that out on their own. Never use a complex word to sound impressive. Never stack multiple frameworks in one sentence. Never say "it's actually more nuanced than that" — just show the nuance.

**Not self-deprecating as a shtick.** Ivan can be honest about what he doesn't know. But don't turn that into a recurring character bit. The audience needs to trust the voice, and constant self-undermining erodes that.

## Vocabulary Rules

**Use freely:** plain English, industry shorthand the audience already knows (CTR, CPM, organic, paid, creative, brief, deck), brand names, specific numbers and dates.

**Use with care:** Ivan's own terminology (e.g., "brand signal," "brand expression"). Introduce through example, define once, then use naturally. Don't repeat the definition every time.

**Avoid completely:** "utilize," "leverage" (as a verb), "ideate," "synergy," "disrupt," "game-changer," "deep dive" (unless ironic), "unpack," "at the end of the day," "it's not about X, it's about Y" (overused structure in marketing content). No em dashes in scripts — use pauses or periods instead.

**Profanity:** Sparingly, for emphasis only. One instance per script maximum. Never in the first 60 seconds. Used to signal "I'm being extra honest here" — not to seem edgy. If in doubt, cut it.

## Structural Patterns for Scripts

**Hook (0-10 seconds):** Start with a specific, concrete claim or question that names the viewer's unspoken suspicion. Not a teaser — a statement. "In 2019, Allbirds was the fastest-growing shoe brand in America. By 2023, they were hemorrhaging money. Here's what went wrong." The viewer should think "yes, I noticed that too" or "wait, what?" within the first sentence.

**Build (body):** Alternate between story and analysis. Tell the story with enough narrative detail that it's entertaining, then pause and explain the mechanism underneath. The story is the vehicle. The strategic insight is the cargo. Neither works without the other.

**Close (final 30 seconds):** Land on a single, portable idea the viewer can take with them. This should be phrased simply enough to repeat to someone else. "The brand that lets you vary the sermon without changing the religion is the one that compounds." The test: would a viewer text this line to a colleague?

---

## Known Failure Modes to Watch For

These are patterns from Ivan's natural delivery that need active management:

1. **Over-transmission.** Too many ideas per sentence. Too many parenthetical tangents. When excited about a concept, Ivan stacks insights faster than the audience can absorb them. In scripts: one idea per beat. If a sentence has a comma followed by a new concept, split it.

2. **Premature jargon.** Introducing proprietary terminology before the audience has been trained to want it. "Semantic distinctiveness framework" means nothing to someone who hasn't seen the problem first. Build the problem first, earn the label later.

3. **Melodrama.** Ivan's natural register can tilt melancholic or grandiose when he's passionate. "The entire edifice of modern marketing is built on a lie" — tone it down. "Most of what gets called brand strategy isn't" — that's the same idea, delivered flat, and it hits harder.

4. **Hedging.** "I'm guessing," "I think maybe," "arguably." Cut these entirely. Either state the claim or flag the uncertainty directly: "I don't have the data on this, but my read is..."

5. **Academic leakage.** Dropping IPA studies, Ehrenberg-Bass citations, Zillmann references, or other academic sourcing in a way that reads like a grad school paper instead of a conversation. The knowledge should inform the script. The citations should stay in the background.

## Reference Lines

These are examples of the voice calibrated correctly. Use as benchmarks:

- "Every brand in a content-heavy category eventually faces the same problem: the thing that gets attention is not the thing that builds the brand."
- "Duolingo didn't get famous because of language learning. They got famous because they let a cartoon owl be unhinged on TikTok. And the reason that works is that the owl IS the brand signal. Everything else is the sermon."
- "The budget went to performance. Performance ate the brand. And then everyone was surprised when the ads stopped working."
- "Strategy isn't the deck you present. It's the filter that tells you what to say no to."
- "Most brands don't have a content problem. They have a coherence problem disguised as a content problem."
